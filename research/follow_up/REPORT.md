# Follow-up on hazard coverage, rare obligations and decision timing

The follow-up preserves the original Track A checkpoint at 23e7f334b83292724d4ef8aa89563b08edd6998f. It adds broader physical and exposure evidence, matched forward specialist tests and a target-free FY2025 protocol. The additional models were evaluated on previously examined FY2019–24 historical outcomes. The experiments support continuing to use Track A as a frozen historical benchmark. They identify a promising high-obligation review signal and leave operational point prediction and uncertainty unresolved.

Rainfall uses NOAA nClimGrid-Daily v1-0-0 and 168 complete monthly county files with version notes. The analysis preserves source hashes, dates and 4,379 county-incident summaries. It uses the highest county daily mean and the largest complete county cumulative total. File dates can follow decision time. Of 520 covered declarations, the last documented source dependency date for all included counties predates the proxy T0 for 27 and T+72 for 30. Some dates refer to historical inputs or reprocessing. These dates do not certify availability of the product, episode geometry or contemporaneous FEMA counties. No final NOAA county rain feature enters the proposed decision-time protocol. [NOAA documentation](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/nclimgrid-daily).

Wind geometry uses azimuthal 34-kt quadrants from HURDAT2 and NOAA IBTrACS US-agency track fields. All four radii must be present. Zero radii may indicate no threshold extent in a quadrant. Use WGS84 forward-geodesic sectors, six-hour fixes and documented additional NHC fixes, then clip the union to designated counties. The 250 km track-to-county check limits matching and can miss winds from distant centers. The method approximates reported wind extents and omits surge, surface roughness, gusts, exposure between fixes and actual damage. [NHC archive](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/data/), [NOAA IBTrACS v4r01](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/international-best-track-archive).

The census overlay apportions 2010 or 2020 Census block group population and housing by the fraction of the group's polygon inside the footprint in EPSG:6933. It supplies fully covered and all-touched bounds for the archived group totals. The estimate assumes uniform within-group density, including polygons with water, and can differ materially from true building or population locations. Of 104 mapped footprints, 81 yielded overlays, 21 received HTML responses in place of Census data, and two lacked usable census attributes. Two of three official endpoint rechecks were explicitly rejected and one returned a data preview. The scored release preserves the original request statuses. Further improvements require a complete independent export or documented smaller queries. [Census TIGERweb](https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Census2020/Tracts_Blocks/MapServer), [Census response manifest](results/spatial/spatial_source_manifest.csv).

The wildfire audit matched official names, dates, state and county intersections using NIFC and MTBS. It allows a retrospective perimeter date after the event within a fixed review window and then records the version and incident ID. It distinguishes mapping all reviewed names from mapping all fires. Some generic wildfire declarations were explicitly selected for document review because they were historically large. Their fire area and housing fields were excluded from every predictive ablation. The supplemental Mawar territory match was also kept out of the model features. SPC path and storm-report endpoints returned HTTP 403 during the audit. NCEI archived report measurements and independent county precipitation remain the quantifiable evidence. [NIFC history](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=e02b85c0ea784ce7bd8add7ae3d293d0), [WFIGS](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=5e72b1699bf74eefb3f3aff6f4ba5511), [verified wildfire name references](event_name_evidence.csv).

## Physical model comparison and $50M review

The physical ablation holds the six purged forward origins, 376 cases and Extra Trees architecture fixed. It preserves a rainfall-covered subgroup of 197 and a wind-population-covered subgroup of 36. An additional check found no newly identified source cyclone shared between used training and held cases in any fold. The rain-plus-wind arm raised raw accuracy from 59.57% to 61.97%, with full-cohort MAE changing from $19.905M to $19.903M. The paired group bootstrap interval for the MAE difference included zero. On the 13 cases at or above $50M, adding both sources changed tail MAE by about $595. This is insufficient evidence of meaningful monetary improvement. ## Forward funding specialists and amount failure

The original least-dollar specialist separates zero obligations from positive amounts. The low and middle bands use separate amount models, including the 900-tree log Extra Trees middle-band implementation. The frozen amount branches also include upper-band medians, a candidate $50–200M overlay, and specialized analogue logic in the original stack. In the original scripts, the outer and some inner fits used fiscal years other than the held year. They also used response features derived from eventual missions. The forward adaptation uses the previously archived earlier-only metadata router on all 376 cases, replaces the historical features, fixes model settings, refits the zero hurdle and three lower-band components inside each fold, and uses medians in the sparse upper bands. It is an architectural transfer with chronological fitting. [Original low-band specialist](../../analysis/nonbio_0_100k_dedicated_ml_specialist.py), [original amount stack](../../analysis/nonbio_final_830_accepted_amount_stack.py).

The nested forward metadata amount underestimated all 13 actual high-dollar cases. Its three largest absolute errors accounted for 83.0% of tail error and 79.0% of full-cohort absolute error. The unconditional training median was zero in each fold and nearly matched overall MAE, which illustrates how weak this metric is on its own for a heavily zero-inflated target. The same router's lower-band specialist adaptation barely changed predictions from its band-median comparator and increased error against nested metadata. The true-band diagnostic shows how much amount error routing might remove. Even with that extra outcome information, within-band error remains large. Across folds the $500M+ class had only 2–3 independent eligible training episodes and the $200–500M class had 4–5. Against a global tail median with known exceedance, partial pooling by hazard did not reduce tail MAE. The probability-weighted prediction improved historical tail MAE and worsened whole-sample MAE by $2.35M. These tests do not support changing the historical or forward amount benchmark. ## Extreme-value uncertainty

Training used independent episode peaks above $5M, $10M and $20M. Only three $5M folds reached the 25-episode fitting gate, with 25–32. All three fitted GPD shapes exceeded 2.2, with unstable bootstrap shape intervals. Their mathematical tail implied very large extrapolation and no finite fitted mean. This indicates model failure for usable intervals on these data. No GPD bound passed the stability screen. [Threshold diagnostics](results/rare_event/tail_gpd_support.csv), [Davison and Smith](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1990.tb01796.x).

The earlier 90% metadata residual intervals again covered 281 of 308 overall and 0 of 9 at or above $50M. Earlier-only dollar residual intervals around the specialists covered 278 of 308 and 0 of 9 high-dollar cases. A stress envelope that extended the upper endpoint to an empirical tail quantile for flagged cases covered 4 of 9 and 294 of 308 overall. It widened the mean interval from $4.00M to $102.28M over all 308 and reached a maximum width of $1.48B. The extension is a review scenario with unvalidated tail coverage. [Intervals and widths](results/rare_event/tail_interval_metrics.csv).

## Historical versus forward performance

On the exact same 376 later cases, the archived Track A gave 88.56% accuracy and $6.48M MAE. The fixed metadata forward model gave 46.01% and $19.86M. Granting that same metadata architecture retrospectively pooled years raised raw accuracy to 52.66%, with $19.85M MAE. Its tail MAE barely changed. A proxy 36-month lag for training availability reduced balanced accuracy from 40.75% to 20.18%. This lag is a sample-size and freshness sensitivity, because the historical dollar targets themselves still lack vintage snapshots.

Updating the fixed forward model with later response counts from the historical workbook raised accuracy to 72.61%, with $18.26M MAE. This demonstrates the predictive information carried by eventual response operations. T+72 action and recent advisory time proxies reached 64.36%, $19.58M MAE and $546.23M tail MAE. The archived 912 cases include 36 with a mission by proxy T0 and 212 by proxy T+72. All 23 historical high-dollar cases had a T+72 action timestamp. All 20,770 archived non-Biological action rows retained by the timing audit had a current refresh after T0, and no linked original declaration title or exact release was certified. Actual operational features require immutable release vintages. The controlled comparisons isolate some factors but cannot assign the whole Track A gap or reproduce its historical tuning under those data restrictions. [Timing metrics](results/timing/decision_time_metrics.csv), [availability audit](results/timing/decision_time_coverage.csv).

## Independent cohort and stopping conditions

The embargoed roster retains 143 unchanged IDs. A metadata-only check confirmed 61 major declarations, 75 Fire Management records and seven Emergency records. The original 912 training records are all major declarations. The comparable primary test population is therefore the 61 FY2025 major declarations. The rest remain reserved for separate applicability analysis. The feature-vintage and obligation-maturity gates are open. There were no FY2025 outcome queries.

The strongest evidence from this round is the increase in retrospective physical source coverage. It also shows that operational labels and tail data are the limiting resources. A new external validation requires genuine as-of sources, documented aggregate label comparability and independent tail episodes. The current evidence supports preserving each challenger and negative result as a research artifact. [FY2025 locked evaluation protocol](FY2025_LOCK.md).
