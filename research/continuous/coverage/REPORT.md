# Historical label coverage and training sensitivity

The source coverage boundary explains 223 silent zero labels in the workbook's earliest three fiscal years. The revised forward input gate treats those labels as unavailable for a separately versioned candidate. The frozen Track A benchmark and all original data and predictions remain preserved.

## Coverage and funding definitions

The historical declaration query retrieved 44 OpenFEMA metadata pages, covering all 1,732 non-Biological major disaster declarations published for FY1995–24. It found 820 FY1995–2009 cases outside the original workbook and matched all 912 FY2010–24 cases with no omitted eligible original-period declaration. All 223 FY2010–12 cases had a zero recorded obligation. Their declarations precede the October 1, 2012 Mission Assignments v2 history boundary. FEMA's publication specifically excludes actions for disasters declared before that boundary. Those zeros cannot be verified as absence of obligations. The earlier declaration cases are useful metadata and add no comparable MA label under the public release. [Roster](audit/declaration_roster.csv), [FEMA dataset documentation via DataLumos](https://www.datalumos.org/datalumos/project/218462/version/V2/view).

An independent DHS Inspector General account documented approximately 2,700 Katrina mission assignments. It substantiates precoverage activity and does not furnish a complete signed, declaration-level action ledger. FEMA's post-Sandy classification change removed the separate Technical Assistance category. Future legitimate additions would require the original action, amendment and reversal history, affected declaration keys, and administrative and time-horizon comparability. Aggregate payments and damage totals answer different questions. [DHS OIG testimony](https://www.oig.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/assets/TM/OIGtm_MJ_022807.pdf), [2018 FEMA Mission Assignment policy](https://emilms.fema.gov/is_0293_es/media/129.pdf).

The exact-ID retrospective snapshot audit used 28 already-known historical declaration IDs. Seven of eight rows sampled from the early recorded-zero class had no public actions. One tracked a two-action net zero. All 20 funded audit cases had public actions. Across the 21 cases with published records, the 6,205 rows had unique action and amendment keys and populated obligation dates. A total of 2,427 were negative adjustments. The current signed-sum amount differed from the workbook by more than one cent in 10 of 21 cases, with a largest absolute difference of **$43.98M**. This stratified sample describes revisions and zero mechanisms. It supplies no population-wide revision or capture rate. [Action and aggregate audit](audit/mission_obligation_audit.csv), [source pages](audit/source_manifest.csv).

Of the 19 audited cases with a 36-month anniversary already elapsed, 12 had a more-than-one-cent difference between the 36-month signed sum and the current signed sum. The largest absolute change after 36 months was **$138.16M**. A common 36-month outcome is a well-defined time endpoint if coverage and date semantics are verified. This audit shows that it can differ substantially from eventual obligations. The data were captured in one current snapshot and cannot prove what was published at the historical cutoffs. The candidate FY2025 target definition and embargo remain under their existing lock. [Historical action audit](audit/mission_obligation_audit.csv), [FY2025 protocol](../../follow_up/FY2025_LOCK.md).

## What the coverage screen changed

The comparison reproduced the exact real-only and fixed 20× controls from the 33-configuration study. All models scored the same 376 real FY2019–24 cases. Screening removed 223 precoverage training examples in each origin while preserving all eligible positive and catastrophic training episodes. The 689 later workbook cases include 349 positive obligation values. In FY2019 the eligible training sample shrank from 514 to 291. One separately fitted binary positive-obligation classifier used the same features and tree settings under both training policies. Results are exploratory.

| Forecast | Training policy | Six-band accuracy | Balanced accuracy | $50M+ AP | Overall MAE | $50M+ MAE |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Unweighted | All archived years | 72.34% | 20.84% | .292 | $19.88M | $553.27M |
| Unweighted | Coverage screened | 73.67% | 25.28% | .282 | $19.92M | $547.57M |
| 20× tail weights | All archived years | 67.29% | 40.03% | .247 | $21.64M | $500.60M |
| 20× tail weights | Coverage screened | 67.02% | 38.04% | .278 | $22.29M | $477.77M |

The binary model's Brier score fell from **.2292 to .2052**. The paired interval for change was −.0482 to −.00005. Its mean probability of a positive obligation rose from .401 to .563, against an observed .479 on the same 376 cases. The coverage screen reduced unweighted $50M+ MAE by $5.71M, with a 95% paired episode interval of −$10.20M to −$2.10M for the change. For fixed 20× weighting, the $22.84M reduction was uncertain (−$70.19M to +$14.06M). Rankings at $200M and $500M were unstable. Fixed weighting after screening reduced pooled $500M+ AP from .418 to .096 on only three events. The data-quality rule is grounded in coverage. Evidence for a new operational classifier or point estimator remains insufficient.
