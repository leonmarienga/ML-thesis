# Transport, disruption and the allocation frontier

The paired replay evaluated 89 policy and floor settings on 48 synthetic cases. Twelve cases came from each combination of USVI survey context and demand distribution. The observed transport regimes were normal in 15 cases, delayed in 18 and closed in 15. The simulated depot uses shared storage and route capacity, service-specific stock and handling requirements, resupply delays and missed in-horizon delivery. Stage-two conditions are either independent planning draws or observations available at second dispatch. All four seed streams are disjoint. Survey anchors only inform household demand. The cargo and disruption parameters are design assumptions. Humanitarian logistics guidance identifies carrying capacity, driving time and road conditions as distinct routing constraints. [Prespecified design](../BATCH14_PROTOCOL.md), [scenario registry](results/scenario_registry.csv), [WFP Logistics Cluster road planning](https://log.logcluster.org/en/sending-goods-road).

Each floor protects a share of the feasible equal-fraction dispatch among currently reachable, stocked cells. Closed routes and missing service stocks exclude a request from the floor but leave it in every outcome denominator. The max-min arm maximizes the minimum coverage attainable among currently reachable zones at each stage, then maximizes delivery within a tolerance of that current-stage solution. The reported least-served-zone outcome is the minimum final service fraction across all eight zones, including closures. Recourse compares expected weighted service, worst possible weighted outcome across four planning draws, and upper-quartile CVaR. The forecast arms share priorities and measurements. Their optimizations do not see realized future demand, resupply or road conditions at the first stage.

The 46 nondominated settings are the empirical frontier of the specified alternatives. The preference examples use weighted sums of the three simulated fractions. They show how a declared preference can change the recommended scenario option. Grey points are dominated in the three reported objectives. [Pareto frontier diagram](results/pareto_frontiers.png), [source points](results/pareto_points.csv), [illustrative preferences](results/preference_choices.csv).

Raising the vulnerability rule's floor from 0% to 100% improved the least-served zone from 8.15% to 31.98%, a paired gain of 23.83 percentage points with a 95% interval from 19.73 to 28.15 points. High-vulnerability service fell by 19.38 points and overall service by 1.42 points. The minimum coverage gain was only 9.07 points in closure cases, versus 39.19 points under normal transport. Under max-min, adding a positive per-cell guarantee may limit the set of feasible reallocations that protects the minimum zone in aggregate. None of worst-branch recourse's sampled settings reached the nondominated set. The frontier contains seven CVaR settings and ten expected-recourse settings. None of the four prespecified preference examples selected a CVaR policy. A preference informed by credible observed routing, demand, dispatch and fulfillment could produce a different decision. Retain the protected choices and the dominated settings as documented simulation evidence. These comparisons estimate trade-offs among explicitly defined algorithms in an artificial network. Actual routing, storage, resource requirements, protected-group outcomes and allocation effects require independently observed system data.
