# Batch 14 transport-aware allocation replay

This supplement freezes implementation choices for the allocation comparison in the Batch 13 protocol before simulation scoring. Use 48 new held cases from the USVI survey contexts and demand distributions, 12 per combination, with separate seed streams for held demand, demand planning, realized network conditions and network planning. Use one depot and eight independently capacity-constrained corridors. Five household request classes have engineering-assumed cargo equivalents of 1, 1.8, 2.5, 0.25 and 2.2 for food, water, electricity access, medication and shelter. Per-unit storage equivalents are 1.0, 1.2, 1.8, 0.4 and 2.8. These are abstract units without physical quantity, dollar cost or actual relief delivery interpretation.

Set nominal supply to 55%, 72% and 90% of the relevant survey anchor, using the fixed design in the replay. Apply capacity variation, delayed resupply and transit losses under normal, delayed and closure regimes. Stage two stocks come from kept initial inventory and independently simulated arrivals. Capped admitted stock and handling volume represent the depot; unadmitted supplies remain outside the depot. Within-stage throughput depends on the capacity of the destination route. A loss in transit represents delivery outside the two-stage horizon. Already delayed requests are tracked as unmet at their due stage. The stage-one optimizer sees current routes, stock and requests and four independent possible futures when recourse is enabled. Realized future routes, delays, stock and requests become available for stage-two dispatch.

Compute an inventory- and route-feasible equal-fraction allocation among reachable, stocked request cells. An unreachable route or unavailable service has no direct floor guarantee and remains in every scored demand denominator. Use a floor of 0, .10, .20 through 1.00 times that stage reference. For planning future reservations, compute the floor conservatively from predicted resupply alone. Include proportional dispatch, total service, vulnerability weight 1+2v, equalized zone need-normalized utility, a mixed 1+v preference and a max-min stage objective with deterministic efficiency tie-breaking. Sequential comparators use the matched vulnerability weight with expected future service, worst-branch weighted unmet need, or upper-quartile CVaR. Evaluate each on matched, independently seeded outcomes.

Use paired bootstrap resampling within survey context and family with 2,000 replicates for the 100% versus 0% floor contrast. Identify the nondominated mean outcomes among the fixed alternatives in all three reported objectives. Illustrate four preference settings as linear weights on comparable service fractions. The preferences are chosen for exposition and do not infer public or operational preferences. Preserve the sorting rule and full frontier candidates. Plot two frontier projections and floor curves by disruption; figures visualize simulated means and do not support real-world policy rankings.
