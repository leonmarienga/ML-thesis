# Earlier declaration announcements and decision-time versions

The National Archives presidential website contains earlier public declaration text relevant to assistance types and named jurisdictions. NARA archives the White House site at the end of each administration. The display date on that frozen site identifies the release claim. Its final preserved body can include subsequent edits. We independently retrieved original-site public captures to test content eligibility. [NARA presidential website policy](https://www.archives.gov/presidential-records/research/archived-white-house-websites), [archived White House](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/), [protocol](../BATCH6_PROTOCOL.md).

## Evidence

The 16 quarterly sampled briefing sitemaps list 106 candidate approval pages. The fixed lexical sample includes 31 notices. Twenty-six match a unique historical FEMA state, effective day and incident onset. Three documents did not yield a complete identity and two parsed identities lacked a unique historical match. None carries recognized original content modification metadata. Under the Eastern civil-day release bound, 24 matched declarations have a claim within 24 hours and all 26 have a claim within 48 hours. Neither figure verifies the availability of the content presented in the later archive. [Sample frame](results/sample_frame.csv), [identity audit](results/document_identity.csv).

For the 12 earliest dated matched identities, the capture check queried the exact original White House URL using the public Internet Archive index. It inspected available successful responses from the inferred release day through the conservative T+72 boundary. A verified replay had to resolve to the exact requested timestamped URL, return an agreeing Memento timestamp and match the same state, effective day and onset in the actual HTML. The six URLs with timely captures yielded 26 verified snapshots. Six queried identities had no capture within the bounded search. One distinct declaration has a verified copy by T+24 and six by T+48. The search can establish a latest possible availability time for each recovered body. It cannot establish the exact signing hour, first publication instant or full archive coverage. [Version manifest and parsed bodies](results/capture_versions.csv), [acquisition manifest](results/source_manifest.csv).

The earliest verified bodies for two of the six declarations differ from the later archived text. For DR4353, an independent capture at 13:30 UTC on January 3, 2018 lists emergency work for Santa Barbara and Ventura counties. The later archived page also lists repair or replacement of damaged facilities. A verified same-day capture at 22:45 UTC includes the expanded wording. For DR4399, the only detected text change adds a cross-reference to the Georgia emergency announcement. The verified assistance clauses are the same. The differences demonstrate that release-day attribution alone can give an early model feature text that was edited later. [DR4353 early version](https://web.archive.org/web/20180103133006id_/https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/president-donald-j-trump-approves-california-disaster-declaration-2/), [DR4353 later capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20180103224509id_/https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/president-donald-j-trump-approves-california-disaster-declaration-2/).
