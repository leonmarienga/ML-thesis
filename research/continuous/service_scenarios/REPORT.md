# Survey-informed essential-service scenarios

The simulator uses weighted USVI CASPER household estimates and survey uncertainty for food, water, utility electricity access and medication. Electricity deficit is the complement of utility availability. The reported response and recovery estimates define independent scenario contexts. The 4%, 8% and 16% shelter rates are assumptions and mark separate sensitivity levels. The national Census Household Pulse Survey supplies qualitative context about adult displacement and shortage. Household request counts are abstract service units, so a request for utility access has no implied wattage, quantity, cost or funding. [USVI source, Table 4](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/114255/cdc_114255_DS1.pdf), [Census and CASPER source screen](../humanitarian_source/REPORT.md).

The experiment generates 48 held scenarios for each combination of survey context and distribution family, for 192 cases. A shared artificial severity shock and fixed vulnerability gradient link the demand directions. A population-weighted intercept separates marginal anchor changes from geographic redistribution. One family perturbs the anchor using its published interval width and simulates binomial counts. The second uses logit-normal anchor uncertainty and beta-binomial zone counts. Both are design sensitivities. The survey does not identify the vulnerability gradient, the severity effect, joint household responses or staged arrival timing. Selected values include 48% first-stage arrivals, 72% nominal supply relative to mean household requests, and 75%–125% realized second-stage replenishment. Supplies are distinct by service and pooled across the eight fictional zones. [Synthetic registry](SYNTHETIC_DATA_REGISTRY.csv), [scenario seeds](results/scenario_registry.csv).

Equal-fraction and vulnerability-sensitive myopic policies dispatch independently as each stage opens. The expected and CVaR variants plan eight future demand and supply realizations from disjoint seeds using a noisy severity signal. The stage-one decisions access realized first-stage demand and available stock. Future observations enter only the validation or the second-stage dispatch. The CVaR objective emphasizes the worst quarter of the assumed simulation loss distribution. The optimized recourse variants compare first-stage decisions under a weighted planning objective with a common vulnerability-sensitive second-stage dispatch. The weighted objective protects high-priority demand and contains no explicit per-zone service floor. The replay checks all dispatches against stage-specific demand and inventory.

The expected and CVaR policies gain less than 0.60 pp of high-vulnerability coverage over the myopic vulnerability policy, with larger reductions in lowest-zone coverage. The major directional trade-off appears under both distribution families in both contexts. The mean equal-fraction recovery coverage falls from 76.28% under one family to 63.56% under the overdispersed family. That difference shows how much recoverability depends on assumptions about unsampled zone counts. The paired intervals compare fixed synthetic cases and quantify Monte Carlo variability within the specified design. They cannot measure real-world policy effects. Retain the transparent myopic rules as simulation comparators. Neither recourse policy enters an operational framework.
