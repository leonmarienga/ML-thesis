# Batch 12 protocol

## Authentic coastal water levels

Freeze a purposive geographic feasibility panel of two listed NOAA CO-OPS sites for each of seven earlier US storms. The center date identifies the earliest nearby coastal-impact date from NHC event descriptions. Measure for three UTC days spanning the center date and the adjacent days. Do not substitute stations after reading levels. Prior outage and wind studies of some storms used other measured endpoints. This panel acquires independently instrumented coastal water level values in place of outage records. It estimates neither representative coastal coverage nor the causal effect of a hazard.

Request exactly two products for each pair, verified hourly height and hourly tide prediction, both in meters with the station's local MSL datum and UTC time. Cap traffic at 28 primary requests with one retry each for transient transport errors, two megabytes per response and six concurrent connections. Require station identity for the observation, a unique time key in each response and no fill of missing hours. Report paired coverage, longest gap and the observation-minus-prediction residual as the non-tidal water-level component. It includes surge, freshwater and other processes. Verified products are retrospective. The current prediction edition has no reconstructed issuance evidence for the historical decision time.

Keep a tide-only baseline and a residual-persistence diagnostic for the one- and six-hour horizons, matched on the same complete evaluated timestamp pair. Run no tuned statistical or synthetic generator. A station-event passes basic coverage at 54 of 72 matched hours. A storm meets the initial physical-source screen when both sites pass. A future transfer experiment requires at least four qualifying earlier storms and two qualifying later storms after any covariate and event-quality screen, with a frozen chronological split and authentic dated pressure, winds or other forcing and bathymetry. The source screen alone authorizes no training.

## Protecting minimum service with sequential allocation

Use newly sampled scenarios sharing the published aggregate anchors and engineering assumptions from Batch 11. Test whether geographically broad service can recover when recourse keeps its priority weights but enforces an interpretable floor. Keep the food, water, electricity, medication and shelter service definitions. Repeat both response and recovery contexts and both distribution families, using 48 new held cases per combination and disjoint random seeds offset from Batch 11 by 100,000. Cross the three shelter values with nominal supply ratios of 55%, 72% and 90% of anchor demand. Assign each combination in a fixed repeating nine-case cycle. All alternatives face the same forecast signals, observed requests and staged stock.

Compare equal-fraction dispatch and a common vulnerable-zone priority objective with expected and upper-quartile CVaR planning. For each objective, compare the matched no-floor policy with a 70% floor relative to the stage's equal-fraction allocation. Include myopic priority with and without the floor. The first-stage floor uses actual stage-one inventory. For the LP's simulated future stage, a conservative lower bound uses forecast resupply alone. Realized stage-two dispatch applies the floor using actually available stock. This leaves the priority objective and observable information fixed. Report paired changes in minimum zone, high-vulnerability and overall coverage under each objective and by supply ratio. The study measures simulated trade-offs under one staged delivery structure. It uses no USDA or FEMA funding amounts, links no individual surveys and requires independently observed demand and delivery before any real-world effectiveness claim.
