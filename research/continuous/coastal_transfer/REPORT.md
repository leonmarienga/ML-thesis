# Storm-level coastal nowcast and synthetic transfer

The preregistered panel uses eight earlier storms, including Florence and Ida from the previous observed panel, and four chronologically held storms in 2024. We acquired 1,723 matched astronomical tide and level hours, 1,435 from ten additional storms. Verified pressure and wind matched 1,480 and 1,470 hours across the panel. All 12 storms passed the two-station water-level and at least one-station forcing rule. Excluding every nonzero NOAA water-level flag removed Ike from the earlier partition and retained all four held storms. Each arm scored the same 542 one-hour and 502 six-hour origins in the primary analysis. The unit of generalization is the storm. Gauge locations and regional regimes can recur across events. The hydrostatic arm relaxes the current residual toward a 1 cm per hPa inverse-barometer equilibrium. The simulation prior adds wind stress in a simplified slab balance with assumed fetch, effective water depth and relaxation away from equilibrium. Approximate landward bearings fix the stress sign convention. The ridge uses only issue-time and three-hour prior residual and forcing inputs. All training weights give each earlier storm the same total weight. The hybrid fits a real-data ridge correction to the synthetic prior, with the same regularization as the real-only ridge. There are 12,000 simulated examples per lead, no historical funding labels and no held inputs or outcomes in the generator. [Inverse-barometer reference](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2021-01/Jason-3%20Products%20Handbook.pdf), [synthetic registry](SYNTHETIC_DATA_REGISTRY.csv).

Real-only ridge improved six-hour MAE by .004 m versus persistence. The paired four-storm bootstrap ranged from a .055 m improvement to a .056 m deterioration. In the unflagged analysis, ridge and hybrid both lost to persistence at six hours. The hybrid also had higher average MAE than real-only ridge at each lead in both flag analyses. The primary storm effects varied, with real-only ridge improving six-hour errors for Debby and Helene and increasing them for Francine and Milton. The data clock here is retrospective, including verified levels and currently served astronomical predictions. A preliminary-gauge issuance check is needed before decision-time use. Four held storms give weak uncertainty resolution and cannot support calibrated local prediction intervals. Preserve the full comparison and both real-only references. The bench test gives no basis for advancing the synthetic prior. A later transfer design can test independent storm regions using measured forcing, coastal geometry, terrain, and unassimilated observations against a validated hydrodynamic model.
