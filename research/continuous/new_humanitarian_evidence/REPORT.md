# Independent needs, receipt, and physical measurement screen

The public Rockaway study reports six separable household results three weeks after Hurricane Sandy. Table 2 reports 28 of 89 households without any electricity source, 12 of 89 using a generator as their current source and 49 of 89 reporting utility service. It also reports 38 of 87 without heat and 18 of 61 reporting donations as a food source in a multiple-response item. Evacuation was reported by 51 of 90 respondents. At most 91 surveys entered the analysis after geographic exclusions, with a 51% overall response rate. These are published sample counts and item denominators. They provide geographic and measurement contrasts with the USVI survey. Reconstructing sampling weights or a housing and service intake panel would require additional access. [Rockaway full-text study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4025720/), [measurement crosswalk](results/measurement_crosswalk.csv).

A separate Rockaway cross-section four months after Sandy describes widespread restored electricity alongside continuing barriers to food and medication. The two Hurricane Michael studies contribute independent local survey samples in Bay and Gulf counties and in Gadsden, Calhoun and Jackson counties. They share a storm. The accessible abstracts describe distinct household impacts and collection periods, including structural damage. Europe PMC advertises subscription access for the detailed reports. These observations support separating preparedness, physical impact and household need. They provide no verified item denominators and uncertainty for matched service-delivery comparisons. CDC also enumerates four Hurricane Maria assessments in Puerto Rico, with unpublished reports. [Rockaway follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30488477/), [Michael Bay and Gulf](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34081673/), [Michael rural counties](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38189201/), [CDC Puerto Rico catalogue](https://www.cdc.gov/casper/php/map/puerto-rico.html).

For a physical observation, NOAA reports a gauge level of 1.2 m above Mean Higher High Water at Pilots Station East during Ida. USGS documents a separate storm-tide deployment. These records motivate a datum-aware physical transfer endpoint, subject to verified timestamps, sensor sites, multiple independent storms and matched exposure. For an outage observation, the EIA records about 96% of Puerto Rico utility customers with restored service by April 2018 using DOE situation reports, alongside depressed net generation. These aggregate metrics use different denominators from household access and off-grid use. The dated source provides an additional check on the measurement definition. No new model is fit on a mixture of those endpoints. [NOAA Ida measured water levels](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/annualreport/2021/hurricane-ida.html), [USGS sensor deployment](https://www.usgs.gov/news/featured-story/usgs-deploying-storm-tide-sensors-louisiana-mississippi-advance-hurricane-ida), [EIA Puerto Rico](https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=42095).

The next independent allocation comparison needs an event and zone key, service-specific eligible population and request denominators, fulfilled quantity and timestamp, assignment or dispatch rule, available supplies and transport constraints. Food source and utility availability are useful separate observational benchmarks. The inspected evidence contains no linked delivery panel suitable for comparing policy effectiveness.
