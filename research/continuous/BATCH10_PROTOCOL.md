# Batch 10 protocol

## Historical signed ledgers

Screen public OpenFEMA action records for the exact precoverage major declaration IDs 1603, 1604, 1786, 1791 and 4020, covering Katrina in Louisiana and Mississippi, Gustav in Louisiana, Ike in Texas and Irene in New York. Use historical declaration identity metadata, a whitelist of action IDs, amendment version, signed amount, obligation date and received date, and at most two 1,000-row pages per ID. Reuse retained postcoverage action records as a positive route control. Inspect original public federal documentation for closeout, signed amendments and cancellations. Inspect the public preservation catalogue at metadata level only. Broad financial exports can contain embargoed outcomes and are outside this audit.

A comparable new target requires an exact declaration, authoritative signed action sequence and effective dates, complete amendment and deobligation coverage, all relevant agencies and programs within scope, and evidence of a complete 36-month elapsed snapshot. A partial report, expense, authorization, reimbursement, zero result returned by an out-of-scope API, or cross-declaration aggregate cannot supply this label. Record alternate source routes and unresolved custodian requirements. No FY2025 financial or other held outcome data may be acquired.

## Outcome-blind storm and county panel

Use the author-verified national EAGLE-I releases already acquired for 2018, 2020, 2022 and 2023. The fixed frame is continental U.S. hurricane-intensity landfalls in those years according to NHC reports. The eleven candidate storms and UTC landfall dates are Florence (2018-09-14), Michael (2018-10-10), Hanna (2020-07-25), Isaias (2020-08-04), Laura (2020-08-27), Sally (2020-09-16), Delta (2020-10-09), Zeta (2020-10-28), Ian (2022-09-28), Nicole (2022-11-10), and Idalia (2023-08-30). Deduplicate multiple mainland landfalls within a storm. Use the first hurricane-intensity continental landfall date, even if an earlier noncontinental or lower-intensity landfall occurred. Verify documentary identity and dates with official NHC tropical cyclone reports. Non-hurricane mainland landfalls are out of scope. The storm, timing and geographic choices use no newly expanded outage outcomes.

Define eligible entry days as two UTC dates before through two UTC dates after the landfall date. Use the 2017 Census county internal points. Consider archived NHC nominal 24-hour valid forecasts within the eligible window whose nominal initialization is at or before UTC midnight beginning the landfall date. Rank counties first if the point is in either the quadrant contour or its equal-area circle at any eligible nominal forecast. Restrict to points at most 400 km from an eligible center, then sort by minimum distance and FIPS, and take up to 60 per storm. Retain the selected county list and a hash on the research branch before extracting EAGLE-I outcome rows. This retrospective design selects a physical sampling frame. It does not certify the original public issuance time of each forecast.

Extract only the selected county and storm time slices from the verified original CSV bytes, including a 48-hour lookback and 24-hour follow-up margin. Reuse the established strict observation screen, observed below-to-high entry rule, 48-hour cooldown and complete-window sustained endpoint. Re-check that observed entry and target dates are in scope. Use a distinct sensitivity excluding explicit zero counts of unresolved provenance. For matching each entry, use the latest available nominal 24-hour forecast valid at or before entry but less than six hours earlier. Retain missing forecasts, county points more than 400 km from the matched center, incomplete follow-up and differences in the two contours. Overlap in counties and hurricane seasons remains a dependence limitation. Separate training through 2020 from held 2022–2023 and identify the four previously used storm groups.

Before a wind-conditioned transfer or generator comparison, require at least four earlier storm groups and two held storm groups. In each qualifying storm require at least 12 complete regional windows across at least four counties, at least three cases of each sustained outcome, and at least two complete observations inside and two outside the quadrant contour. Report whether support also survives excluding unverified zero rows and excluding the previously studied storm groups. Freeze a further generator comparison only if the available support and target-use protocol justify it. Preserve the real-only Batch 8 comparators and never reuse held outcomes for generation. The auxiliary target is reported county outage recovery, distinct from physical wind, damage, humanitarian need and obligations.

## Independent humanitarian observations

Acquire the public CDC US Virgin Islands 2017–2018 CASPER published report and the official Census Household Pulse Survey housing-table releases for weeks 52, 61, and 62. Review official FEMA shelter service layer metadata for historical timestamp, versioning, geographic keys, observed occupancy, capacity and comparability. Bound to public report tables, metadata and three waves. Retain verified weighted needs, deprivation and displacement observations with their units, response populations and timing. Audit whether any source supplies event-resolved pre-allocation demand, observed delivery, eligibility and an evaluable allocation comparator. Contextual prevalence and confirmed service utilization may inform distinct simulation targets. They do not confer causal evidence for an allocation policy. No individual or FY2025 data is in scope.
