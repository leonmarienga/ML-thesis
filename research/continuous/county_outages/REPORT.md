# County outage and municipal duration source audit

The official EAGLE-I distribution links to Globus. The anonymous transfer endpoint requires credentials. Its authors also released the underlying observations on Figshare and documented the file semantics in Scientific Data. The pilot verified all 999,210,542 bytes of the annual 2018 file against the published MD5. It then retained exactly 657,547 source rows for the 24 fixed counties. The published timestamp is UTC. EAGLE-I omits customer counts of zero and the authors state that a missing row can indicate either zero or lost collection. [ORNL catalogue](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1975202), [author release](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.24237376.v1), [publisher documentation](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-024-03095-5).

## County outage endpoint

The valid series contains 646,178 observations after dropping 532 off-grid clocks and 10,837 in-grid rows with missing or nonnumeric counts. No conflicting or identical duplicate county-time counts were found. No observed zero appears in the retained sample. County quarter-hour availability ranges from 38.6% to 99.0%, with median 82.2%. These figures describe usable reported observations. They do not identify whether the reporting feed was continuously functioning. [Clock and count audit](results/county_clock_audit.csv).

The fixed rule found 5,513 threshold entries with an observed prior quarter below 1,000 and a current quarter at or above 1,000. Another 336 high snapshots had an unknown predecessor. These rows cannot establish incident onset. There are 3,471 complete 24-hour windows, or 63.0% of verified entries. They span 26 two-week and 13 four-week calendar blocks. Entries include recurring changes within counties and storms. The blocks approximate dependence, and neither the entry count nor the block count certifies independent disasters. [Locked protocol](../BATCH7_PROTOCOL.md), [threshold windows](results/county_threshold_windows.csv).

Among the 3,471 complete windows, 3,447 observed a drop below 1,000 within 24 hours. Only 2,771 were still below the threshold exactly 24 hours after onset, and 2,713 showed a renewed high count following an observed drop. The endpoint is highly sensitive to the definition of recovery. A recorded drop within 24 hours can also establish the affirmative endpoint in an incomplete window. Counting only affirmative observations establishes a lower bound of 99.42% among verified entries. Treating the eight unresolved cases as affirmative gives an upper bound of 99.56%. These are descriptive bounds for the selected entries. They cannot characterize entries with unknown onset, all utility outages or individual household restoration. [County summary](results/county_window_summary.csv).

A continuous window can establish an observed customer-count trajectory. The pilot covers one year, only 24 complete windows lack a verified drop, and recrossings are common. These constraints preclude the intended forward or synthetic-transfer comparison on an unambiguous restoration endpoint. A future chronological analysis would need additional independent years, storm-level grouping, a sustained recovery definition, and evidence about utility feed coverage and observed zeros.

## Independent point-event source

The City of Mesa publishes a redacted APPA eReliability outage export. The historical query retrieved 18 rows, all from February–June 2020, and 17 distinct event identifiers. All elapsed start and end clocks were positive and agreed with the reported durations to within one minute under the minutes interpretation. Two records reported at least 1,000 customers affected. None exceeded 24 elapsed hours or had an explicit weather label in its recorded cause. The small export provides an independent timestamp and units comparison. Its scope cannot validate a weather-related model or county-level coverage. [Municipal catalogue](https://citydata.mesaaz.gov/d/iyqi-xjyw), [event summary](results/municipal_summary.csv), [cause audit](results/municipal_cause_counts.csv).

This source workstream produced no synthetic records. Its contribution is a reproducible screen for future outage simulation or auxiliary learning, while the monetary and allocation task definitions retain their own evidence requirements.
