# AI-Assisted Decision-Support Framework for Disaster Relief Resource Allocation Under Uncertainty

## Research question

Can hazard-specific severity and exposure evidence improve prediction of historical FEMA obligations, and how can uncertain estimates and independently elicited humanitarian needs support resource-allocation decisions? The study evaluates retrospective obligation prediction, decision-time evidence availability, and simulated allocation performance as separate empirical questions.

## Methods

**Cohort and targets.** The frozen 2010–2024 workbook has 971 unique disaster declarations. Excluding 59 Biological records yields the 912-case evaluation population. The target is the workbook's total FEMA mission-assignment obligated funding, with negative net totals clipped to zero under the original cleaning convention. Six band intervals are lower-inclusive at $0, $100K, $1M, $50M, $200M and $500M, with the highest band open above. The data do not measure requested funding, realized economic loss or service need. The original router was reconstructed only through original prediction artifacts. The original leave-fiscal-year-out protocol allows later years in training and measures historical reconstruction.

**Flood water change.** A geography-defined design fixes 63 original county anchors and adds four previously sampled DR4722 locations for a six-window sensitivity case. For each window the algorithm checks raster alignment and validates class codes. For month pairs (a,b), a gain is dry-to-water and a loss is water-to-dry. Zero denotes insufficient observation. The primary mask is the intersection of observed pixels on all four dates, with at least 30% window coverage. The comparison subtracts the prior-year seasonal transition from the event-year transition. Geodesic pixel-area weights yield net square kilometres per 100 observable square kilometres. Sensitivities use 60% and 80% thresholds, pair-specific denominators, 2024 interannual transitions and removal of DR4722. December 2021 pixels are ineligible when linked to a January 2023 pair, because the edition differs. NASA GIBS MODIS composites and USGS daily gauges provide independent sensor and hydrologic context. Rendered colors identify visible flood classifications, while transparency can encode different missing or dry states.

**Hazard linkage and exposure.** Each FEMA geography is matched on ID, current state and incident dates with valid county FIPS. NOAA county observations must overlap the incident dates and allowed event types. Reports qualifying for several FEMA IDs are excluded from aggregation. NOAA measurement type separates measured gusts, estimated gusts and hail. NHC best tracks match archived official storm names and track fixes within incident dates and 250 km of designated geography. MTBS wildfire ignition dates and names are checked against FEMA titles and perimeters intersecting designated counties. Earthquakes are screened on incident date and epicenter within the same designated area. ComCat magnitude types remain explicit. NASA Daymet precipitation and previously examined USGS anomalies enter only exploratory arms. County population is a retrospective administrative exposure proxy. Validated hazard-specific depth or asset exposure had insufficient coverage for cohort-level modeling.

**Prediction and validation.** Track B scores each FY2019–24 origin using earlier fiscal years, with training incident end before the earliest held-out event date and shared event and source groups removed. Source-connected groups use repeated NHC storms, NOAA episodes, MTBS and ComCat IDs, and overlapping same-state/hazard incidents. Grouping reduces documented overlaps. Undetected physical connections may remain. Training-only imputation includes missingness indicators and one-hot encoding. The amount model estimates log1p dollars internally and back-transforms. Inner validation uses the two latest earlier fiscal-year origins to select band model by six-label macro F1 and amount model by untransformed MAE. Fixed-architecture ablation, regularized linear models, Extra Trees, Random Forest and histogram gradient boosting, supported hazard experts, hard and soft band specialists, hybrid medians, equal averaging and an OOF-trained linear blend were compared. The models exclude monetary and derived funding fields. Paired bootstrap resamples connected event groups for uncertainties on absolute dollar error and routing accuracy differences. Exploratory variant rankings reuse a historical population and should be interpreted accordingly.

**Decision-time availability.** Finalized NOAA Storm Data, HURDAT2, MTBS and final FEMA designated areas have no point-in-time certification and are excluded from any real-time claim. Earlier repository tests applied estimated declaration-time and T+72 actions cutoffs to historical missions and contemporaneous NHC advisory issue times. Those records lack the original FEMA versions and exact issuance timestamps. The 143-case FY2025 list reserves an independent target-free cohort, subject to maturity and source verification. No FY2025 funding was queried.

**Uncertainty and review.** For each forward origin, the calibration set contains residuals from earlier already forward-tested years, excluding shared event groups. For nominal coverage $1-\alpha$, use the order statistic at rank (\lceil(n+1)$1-\alpha$\rceil) in dollar or log1p absolute residuals. Clip lower bounds at zero and evaluate nominal 80% and 90% intervals by band and year. The method assumes exchangeability for a formal coverage guarantee. Temporal changes in this study restrict the claims to empirical performance. Maximum band probabilities, interval widths, calibrated errors, unavailable features and rare bands inform review flags. Groupwise matched ablations provide feature interpretation rather than causal attribution.

**Resource allocation.** The simulator draws need from exposure, a synthetic severity and vulnerability context, hazard service intensity and stochastic need variation. Need is expressed in service units, with budget increments in compatible units. The candidate decision rules use currently arrived events and budgets, and never receive realized need or future arrivals. Simulated performance records served fraction, vulnerability-weighted coverage, lower-tail service, unmet need accumulated over time, and spending waste. The efficient policy maximizes expected coverage by assigning marginal segments of the piecewise-linear need distribution. The equity policy reserves up to 35% of each remaining wave budget for baseline service and prioritizes vulnerability in the objective. The risk-tilted policy upweights the high-need scenario. A human chooses objectives and reviews recommendations.

## Results

The frozen historical benchmark reproduced 830 correct routes on 912 records with $5.015M MAE and R² 0.8868 for the reported overlay. Within the same amount archive, the original stack MAE was $5.131M and the accepted two-low-specialist version had $5.128M MAE. These differ because the $50–200M historical overlay changes 13 predictions.

The Phase 50 seasonal placebo retained 44 of 63 anchors at 30% common observability, representing 27 disaster IDs. Gains were more frequent per observed area in the event transition, but losses exceeded gains. Net signed change was −0.1103 in the event transition and −0.0531 in the seasonal control. The paired median across disasters was zero. Phase 49 reconstructions exactly matched the archived transitions at all 67 sampled windows. DR4722 had positive independently rendered evidence in two Houston County windows and high regional streamflow in Aitkin. Surface-weather variability, sensor geometry and missingness restrict extrapolation.

Within 376 matched forward cases, the verified-link feature arm increased raw Extra Trees accuracy by 8.51 percentage points against retrospective metadata while losing 9.33 points in balanced accuracy. The cluster bootstrap for dollar MAE difference spans zero. The nested metadata model gave macro F1 .3368 and $19.91M MAE. Geographic and physical additions and alternative integration strategies did not yield a consistent result across error, equity of recall and extremes. The FY2019–24 test population contained 13 actual cases of $50M or more. Archived near-decision results on the same fiscal years showed uplift from mission activity and little evidence of a marginal NHC MAE gain. [Earlier cutoffs experiment](../analysis/EARLY_RESPONSE_912_T0_T72_RESULTS.md).

The nested metadata dollar intervals achieved 84.42% and 91.23% coverage across 308 later cases, with median widths $0.749M and $4.283M. At 90%, all nine cases of $50M or more were missed. Original CQR intervals covered 95.13% nominally 90% across the same size broader period, with conditional high-band intervals around billions of dollars and six of nine high-band outcomes covered. Chronological residual methods cannot remove uncertainty caused by a shortage of extreme observations.

In a held-out-input sensitivity check, perturbing verified linked wind by ±10% changed no routes among 48 covered cases, while the 90th percentile of absolute amount change was $79,523–$94,025. Perturbing designated county population by ±20% changed 1.0%–1.7% of routes among 293 covered cases. Only one test case had a named MTBS single-fire feature. The perturbations were prescribed sensitivity ranges, with no inferred stochastic sensor-error model.

In the high-noise sequential allocation scenario with a planned 65% budget, equal allocation reached 45.43% demand served, expected efficiency 49.69%, and expected equity 49.59%. The equity rule raised coverage of the higher-vulnerability group from 52.72% to 62.33% compared with expected efficiency and reduced weighted waiting. The tail-weighted policy increased higher-vulnerability service to 65.22% while reducing overall service to 48.83%. Sampling uncertainty and sensitivity by budget and arrival regime are available in the scenario results.

## Discussion and limitations

Historical obligations primarily reflect historical operations and administrative processes. Physical severity and exposure have a plausible role in allocating relief, but linkage depends on observation and match quality, and research inputs have uneven coverage across hazards. Hazards that drive rare financial extremes remain difficult to predict. A higher overall classification accuracy can conceal missed smaller classes and poor amount estimates.

The archived workflow and the new audits support a defensible retrospective framework with human-review flags. Exact as-of reconstruction, field-level event attribution, representative inundation and asset exposure, direct humanitarian need labels, and untouched decision-time validation remain unfulfilled. The research can demonstrate sensitivity and compare hypothetical priorities, with operational use requiring subsequent evidence.

The study contributes a fully specified audit trail linking NASA/JRC water classes and observability to the existing FEMA reconstruction, a matched hazard-feature comparison with episode purging, and a simulation with independently defined need. Prior work establishes flood mapping at scale, interval methods under distribution shift, and humanitarian deprivation measures. This thesis applies those established methods in a documented FEMA case study. [Pekel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20584), [Tellman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03695-w), [Barber et al.](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2276), [Holguín-Veras et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jom.2013.06.002).

## Future research

Register source and outcome dates and fix a comparable obligation horizon before opening FY2025 targets. Acquire 2025 original historical feature snapshots and confirm physical episode and declaration overlaps. Test a single predefined rule against a metadata baseline with paired outcomes and a rare-band review mandate. For flooding, retrieve authorized raw MCDWD files and quality masks, validate event-scale footprints with independent georeferenced observations, then estimate spatial vulnerability and infrastructure exposure. Validate the allocation objective with emergency managers and measured service outcomes before any operational trial.
