## Adaptations and limits

The NASA historical MODIS archive provided valid raw granule listings and returned HTTP 303 access redirects for two tested HDF assets. Public, documented GIBS tiles preserved exact visible palette values, which allowed an independent sensor check without accessing gated raw files. The report records dates, source hashes, individual windows and transparent cells. At least 896 of 5,407 JRC gain centers at Houston window 2 and 1,279 of 2,288 at window 3 coincided with MODIS flood-class cells on April 20, 2023. Positive overlap also occurred April 10 and April 25. The 2022 spring render had insufficient data for the tested Houston windows. A 2024 control showed some positive overlap at window 2. These inputs cannot establish measured false-positive rates.

The original reinforcement learning notebook was reviewed for sequential budget design. Its reward uses the known historical funding label, so its performance does not measure humanitarian service. The new simulator retains staged arrivals and budgets and defines needs independently. The source checks and selected classical and tree models fit the sample size. Support for separate small-hazard models, learned deep ensembles, and a validated operational allocation policy is absent.

## Input sensitivity

Held-out perturbations to the verified-link Extra Trees candidate used fixed training sets and model settings. A ±10% wind stress changed no classifications among 48 measured cases and shifted 90th-percentile estimated dollars by $79,523–$94,025. A ±20% designated population stress changed 1.0%–1.7% of routes among 293 covered cases. One MTBS-covered test record is insufficient for a useful wildfire sensitivity estimate. The perturbations are sensitivity scenarios, with no independently estimated measurement-error distribution.
