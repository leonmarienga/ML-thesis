# Frozen six-band FEMA obligations benchmark and historical DQN crosswalk

Evidence pinned to [`b0b6edb2`](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/tree/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6). This research distinguishes archived performance from out-of-time validation and uses only FY2010–24 historical evidence.

## Manuscript-ready subsection

Track A is an immutable reconstruction of an exploratory historical FEMA Mission Assignment obligations pipeline. The frozen workbook contains 971 unique declarations from FY2010–24; excluding 59 Biological declarations leaves 912 observations. The archived response is the workbook's total obligated amount in USD, with negative net amounts clipped to zero under the historical convention. Six classes use cutoffs of $0, $100,000, $1 million, $50 million, $200 million, and $500 million. A value on a cutoff enters the higher band except zero, and the last band is unbounded. These accounting labels are neither economic losses nor humanitarian need. Of the 912 rows, 223 before the public October 2012 coverage boundary have workbook zeros; they remain part of the frozen replay but are not certified administrative zeros or admissible coverage-screened training labels absent a comparable complete ledger. [Methods](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/blob/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6/research/THESIS_METHODS_RESULTS_DISCUSSION.md) · [Coverage discussion](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/blob/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6/research/FRAMEWORK.md)

The pipeline separates the **six-band router**, which produces a categorical prediction, from a **band-specific dollar estimator**, which uses the predicted band. The replay loads and reconciles archived outer leave-fiscal-year-out (LFYO) predictions rather than fitting a new router. It checks one-to-one declaration-ID matching, equal fiscal years and observed targets, expected route membership, and finite band-specific amounts. The original dollar stack, the accepted low-band overlay, and the later $50–200 million research overlay have identical predicted bands, so their historical 830/912 correct routes (91.01%) cannot be attributed to the amount overlays. The more favorable $5.015 million MAE belongs specifically to the research-only $50–200 million overlay, not the original or accepted-low-band stack. [Reconstruction code](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/blob/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6/analysis/nonbio_final_50_200m_immutable_overlay.py) · [Archived replay](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/actions/runs/41397678781) · [Metrics](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/blob/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6/research/results/track_a/model_metrics.csv)

**Important validity boundary.** LFYO isolates the nominal held fiscal year during component fits; it is not chronological, and the preserved artifact is not a target-blind prediction benchmark. Several accepted router rule audits construct the test-eligible hazard subset with the row's true historical obligation `target_clean < 50_000_000` before selecting the held year. Thus the true outcome controls eligibility for a prediction modification even when threshold estimation and inner LFYO selection used training rows only. Later amounts that rely on this router inherit that limitation. The retrospective rules also use final mission/mission-amendment or duration records without certified historical release-time provenance. Report 830/912 as an immutable historical accounting result, **not** an unbiased out-of-sample or real-time accuracy estimate. No counterfactual accuracy under target-blind gates is claimed. Distinguish the explicit “oracle band” amount diagnostic from the predicted-band stack; do not report oracle errors as application performance. The thesis's separate Track B checks earlier-only, episode-purged origin validation, but its previously examined years and feature-vintage limitations likewise preclude operational claims. ## DQN was an allocation experiment, not the archived router

The [pinned reinforcement learning notebook](https://github.com/leonmarienga/ML-thesis/blob/b0b6edb229d112dd6d11896a512bf1112414e4f6/reinforcement_learning.ipynb) treats the base ML amount as input to a sequential budget adjustment policy. Its six observation entries encode the log-scaled ML amount and five budget-state ratios or scales. Actions are multipliers (0.40, 0.55, 0.70, 0.85, 1.00, 1.10); the environment clips each recommendation to the unspent released budget. Historical funding is the reward reference, not a disaster feature delivered to the agent. The main reward is negative absolute log-dollar error, with additional penalties for spending outside constraints, recommendations below 25% of positive historical funding, budget pressure and terminal unused funds.

The pinned notebook's tuned clean DQN was selected by average reward and reported **−191.8738**, versus **−319.5844** for ML-only. Its raw dollar MAE worsened (**$15.44M vs $14.30M**) and its mean episode count of recommendations below 25% of historical funding declined (**11.83 vs 20.00**). All six final policies recorded zero overspend, consistent with the hard environment cap. The compared policies included random multipliers, a hand-coded rule, ML-only, tuned DQN, A2C and PPO. The clean DQN used 100,000 timesteps, learning rate 0.0003, gamma 0.995 and the selected 0.45 exploration fraction. Each reported evaluation used 30 seeded, nominally 200-scenario episodes sampled from the same `RL_TRAINING_DATA_PATH` used to train and tune the policies; this compares simulation orders, not a held-out disaster population. Code applies budget releases at pages 1, 15, 32, 54, 79, 108, 136, and 160 in the full design, but a 200-scenario episode at five scenarios per page reaches only pages 1, 15 and 32. Keep the 875-scenario UI or any earlier reward table separate unless the exact supporting run is recovered. This evidence is not an observed human-versus-AI or causal humanitarian impact comparison. Later thesis simulations measure independently specified service needs and equity/workload trade-offs rather than closeness to historical dollar obligations.
